About

I am a PhD student in Computer Science at the University of Virginia (expected May 2030), working in the intersection of AI, mobile sensing, and mental health. My research asks how digital signals from everyday devices — phones, wearables, and social media — can help us understand human behavior and mental health as it actually unfolds in the real world.

I am interested in building context-aware AI systems that can detect early signs of distress and deliver support that fits a person's real situation — from clinical interventions for serious mental illness to lightweight tools for student wellbeing. I am also drawn to the methodological questions underneath: what behavioral signals actually generalize across people and settings, and where current models fall short.

Before UVA, I completed my B.A. in Computer Science at Bard College (May 2025), where I was a Distinguished Scientist Scholar. My prior work spans agent-based modeling, NLP evaluation pipelines, acoustic feature analysis for health prediction, and software automation — all of which feed into my interest in building systems that reason about people and their contexts.
