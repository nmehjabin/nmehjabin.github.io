Nadia Mehjabin

PhD Student in Computer Science · University of Virginia

I am a PhD student in Computer Science at the University of Virginia (expected May 2030), working at the intersection of AI, mobile sensing, and mental health. My research asks how digital signals from everyday devices — phones, wearables, and social media — can help us understand human behavior and mental health as it actually unfolds in the real world.

I am interested in building context-aware AI systems that detect early signs of distress and deliver support that fits a person's real situation. I am also drawn to the methodological questions: what behavioral signals generalize across people and settings, and where current models fall short.

Before UVA, I completed my B.A. in Computer Science at Bard College (May 2025), where I was a Distinguished Scientist Scholar. My prior work spans agent-based modeling, NLP evaluation pipelines, acoustic feature analysis for health prediction, and software automation.

Research

Modeling the Extra-Cellular Matrix in Cancer Angiogenesis

Senior Thesis · Bard College · May 2025

Developed an agent-based model of triple-negative breast cancer integrating a dynamic ECM module and gradient-responsive migration algorithm. Demonstrated that radial ECM gradients increase cellular displacement by ~114% compared to uniform gradients, and validated the synergistic role of VEGF and ECM interactions in driving angiogenesis.

Acoustic Features for Neurobehavioral Health Prediction

Data Science Intern · Vocadian Inc. · Summer 2023

Identified acoustic features in human speech using OpenSmile to predict neurobehavioral performance degradation in Spanish-speaking mining workers. Conducted research on voice as a non-invasive digital biomarker for sleepiness, stress, and Parkinson's disease monitoring in ambulatory settings.

Nanocrystal Benchmarking Pipeline

Research Fellow · Bard Summer Research Institute · Summer 2023

Built an automated end-to-end data pipeline in Python to generate benchmarking reports for nanocrystal samples using the Materials Project database. Created a Tkinter GUI to visualize and rank nanocrystal structures by Pearson correlation coefficients.

Neural Text Simplification & Human Evaluation

Research Fellow · Bard Summer Research Institute · Summer 2022

Designed an end-to-end evaluation pipeline using the Amazon Mechanical Turk API to distinguish model-generated text from human references in sentence fusion and text simplification tasks. Leveraged Pandas, Scikit-learn, and Matplotlib to analyze agreement metrics and optimize model performance.

Personal Project · April 2022

Built an agent-based model in Python simulating COVID-19 transmission across indoor environments. Demonstrated that N95 masks reduce infection rates significantly more than surgical masks, while modeling the effects of environmental dynamics and user behavior on viral spread.
